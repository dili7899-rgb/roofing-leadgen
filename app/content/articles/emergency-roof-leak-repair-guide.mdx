# Emergency Roof Leak Repair Guide 2026: What to Do Before More Damage Occurs

If water is entering your home **right now**, do not start by trying to find the hole on the roof.

Start with life safety.

A roof leak can create electrical hazards, ceiling-collapse risk, saturated insulation, damaged roof decking, and hidden water migration several feet away from the visible stain.

## If Your Roof Is Leaking Right Now: Do These 7 Things

1. **Keep people away from sagging or bulging ceilings.**
2. **Check whether water is near lights, outlets, fans, wiring, or the electrical panel.**
3. **Move furniture, electronics, rugs, and valuables out of the wet area if it is safe.**
4. **Place buckets or containers beneath stable dripping areas.**
5. **Take photos and video before disturbing damaged materials.**
6. **Do not climb onto a wet, icy, wind-damaged, or storm-exposed roof.**
7. **Call an emergency roofing contractor if exterior containment is needed.**

> **Immediate Safety Trigger:** If water is coming through an electrical fixture, the ceiling is visibly sagging, you hear cracking, or roof framing has been hit by a tree, leave the affected area and obtain professional assistance.

# 2026 Emergency Roof Leak Priorities

During an active leak, use a **hazard hierarchy** rather than immediately searching for the exterior defect.

| Priority                   | Question                                                                                           | Immediate action                                            |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Life safety**         | Is there electrical exposure, ceiling collapse risk, structural damage, or a gas-appliance hazard? | Isolate the area and call qualified help                    |
| **2. Active weather**      | Is rain, hail, lightning, ice, or strong wind still occurring?                                     | Remain off the roof                                         |
| **3. Water containment**   | Can dripping water be safely captured?                                                             | Use containers and protect contents                         |
| **4. Documentation**       | Could storm damage or insurance be involved?                                                       | Photograph before temporary work                            |
| **5. Exterior mitigation** | Can trained workers safely access the roof?                                                        | Tarp or temporarily patch the damaged area                  |
| **6. Permanent diagnosis** | Where is the actual entry point?                                                                   | Inspect flashing, shingles, penetrations, valleys, and deck |
| **7. Drying**              | Which materials became saturated?                                                                  | Begin extraction and drying promptly                        |

The goal is simple:

**Protect people → stop additional water → preserve evidence → dry the structure → complete the permanent repair.**

### What Professional Roof Tarping Costs in 2026

A normal professional tarp installation commonly runs about:

**$0.70–$2.00 per square foot**

Emergency or after-hours tarping can reach approximately:

**$1.00–$2.80 per square foot**

For example, covering 600 square feet of damaged roof at emergency rates could cost roughly:

`600 × $1.00–$2.80 = $600–$1,680`

Whole-roof or difficult storm-loss projects can cost substantially more.

### RCV vs. ACV Does Not Make a Tarp Automatically Covered

Do not assume that an RCV policy automatically reimburses every emergency contractor charge.

Temporary mitigation and permanent roof settlement are separate issues.

**Replacement Cost Value (RCV)** generally pays the covered cost of repair or replacement using like-kind materials without permanently deducting depreciation, subject to the policy.

**Actual Cash Value (ACV)** generally pays replacement cost minus depreciation.

Temporary tarping may be treated as a reasonable mitigation expense when the underlying loss is covered, but reimbursement still depends on:

* Cause of loss
* Policy terms
* Deductible
* Reasonableness of the charge
* Documentation
* Coverage limits
* Applicable exclusions

Keep every receipt.

# 2026 Code Trigger: Understand IRC R908 Before Someone Quotes the "25% Rule"

> **2026 Code Trigger — IRC R908:** There is **no universal nationwide IRC rule stating that damage to 25% of a residential roof automatically requires complete roof replacement**.

IRC Section R908 addresses **reroofing**, including requirements for roof replacement and roof recovering.

Under the 2024 IRC, roof replacement generally involves removal of existing roof-covering layers down to the roof deck, subject to applicable exceptions.

States and local jurisdictions can adopt:

* Different IRC editions
* State amendments
* Local reroofing provisions
* Special hurricane requirements
* Existing-building provisions
* Roof-section definitions

Some jurisdictions have used or modified **25% thresholds**, but those requirements must be verified locally.

### If a Contractor Says "Code Requires the Whole Roof"

Ask for:

1. **The exact adopted code section**
2. **The code edition**
3. **Any state or local amendment**
4. **How the jurisdiction defines the affected roof section**
5. **Whether an exception applies**

A verbal statement that "anything over 25% has to be replaced" is not enough.

# Active Leak Insurance Trigger: Document Before You Change the Evidence

> **Insurance Documentation Trigger:** If wind, hail, falling debris, or another potentially covered event caused the leak, document the condition **before temporary repairs whenever safely possible**.

Insurance guidance generally expects homeowners to take reasonable steps to prevent additional damage.

That does not mean delaying an emergency tarp while rain continues entering the structure.

It means documenting the condition first **when doing so does not create additional risk**.

## Use the Three-Level Photo Method

For every damaged location, take:

### 1. Wide Context Photo

Show the entire:

* Room
* Roof plane
* Attic area
* Exterior wall
* Ceiling

This establishes location.

### 2. Mid-Range Photo

Show the damage relative to:

* Vent
* Chimney
* Valley
* Ridge
* Skylight
* Wall intersection
* Tree impact

### 3. Close-Up Photo

Capture the actual:

* Missing shingle
* Puncture
* Crack
* Broken flashing
* Wet deck
* Ceiling damage
* Fastener failure
* Hail impact

Take **before-and-after photos** of temporary work.

# Immediate Interior Mitigation: What to Do Inside the House

Exterior repairs can wait until weather conditions are safe.

Interior water damage cannot.

## Step 1: Clear the Fall Zone

Move people and pets away from:

* Sagging drywall
* Ceiling bulges
* Wet light fixtures
* Cracking ceiling seams
* Areas beneath damaged rafters or trusses

Do not stand directly under a water-filled ceiling simply to position a bucket.

---

## Step 2: Protect Contents

If the room is safe to enter, move:

* Electronics
* Furniture
* Rugs
* Artwork
* Clothing
* Documents
* Mattresses

Use plastic sheeting to protect heavy items that cannot easily be moved.

Do not run extension cords through wet areas.

---

## Step 3: Capture Stable Dripping Water

For a stable ceiling with a simple drip, use:

* Wide rigid bucket
* Secondary overflow container
* Plastic floor sheeting
* Absorbent towels

A large container is better than repeatedly emptying a small bucket during heavy rainfall.

### Track the Approximate Water Volume

Water weighs approximately **8.34 pounds per gallon**.

That means:

| Water volume | Approximate weight |
| -----------: | -----------------: |
|    2 gallons |           16.7 lb. |
|    5 gallons |           41.7 lb. |
|   10 gallons |           83.4 lb. |
|   20 gallons |          166.8 lb. |

That weight matters when water becomes trapped above drywall.

The ceiling is also carrying saturated gypsum and potentially wet insulation.

# Should You Make a Relief Hole in a Wet Ceiling?

A ceiling relief opening can sometimes reduce the weight of a **small, localized pocket of water**.

It is not appropriate for every leak.

## Do NOT Puncture a Ceiling When:

* The bulge is large or rapidly expanding
* Drywall is cracking
* Drywall is separating from framing
* Water is emerging through a light
* A fan or smoke alarm is involved
* Wiring location is unknown
* The floor is wet near energized equipment
* Structural framing may be damaged
* Wet insulation may fall
* You would have to stand beneath the unstable area

In those situations, **leave the room and restrict access**.

---

## Controlled Relief of a Small, Stable Water Pocket

Only consider controlled drainage when:

* The ceiling is otherwise stable
* The pocket is small and localized
* No electrical component is involved
* Wiring is not expected at the drainage point
* You can remain outside the direct fall zone

A cautious sequence is:

1. Remove people and valuables.
2. Place a wide container beneath the lowest point.
3. Protect the floor.
4. Verify no fixture or known wiring is involved.
5. Stay outside the direct fall path.
6. Create only a small drainage opening at the lowest point.
7. Allow water to drain gradually.
8. Stop immediately if the ceiling cracks or begins separating.
9. Leave the area open for inspection and drying.

If you cannot confidently rule out electrical or collapse hazards, **do not make the opening yourself**.

# Water Coming Through a Light Fixture Is an Electrical Emergency

Treat these conditions as electrical hazards:

* Water dripping from a ceiling light
* Wet recessed lighting
* Water around a fan
* Wet switches or outlets
* Buzzing
* Arcing
* Burning smell
* Water near the electrical panel
* Wet exposed wiring

## Do Not:

* Touch the fixture
* Remove the bulb
* Touch a wet switch
* Stand in water while operating a breaker
* Touch the electrical panel with wet hands
* Assume a tripped breaker means all wiring is safe

If the appropriate circuit can be safely de-energized from a **completely dry and safely accessible panel**, it may be switched off.

If the panel or surrounding floor is wet, arcing is occurring, or you cannot confidently identify the circuit, contact a qualified electrician, utility, or emergency service as appropriate.

# Why the Ceiling Stain May Be Far From the Roof Leak

Do not assume the exterior hole is directly above the interior drip.

After water gets beneath the primary roof covering, it can move:

* Downslope along the roof deck
* Along rafters or trusses
* Across flashing
* Along plumbing pipes
* Along electrical cable
* Through insulation
* Across vapor-control layers
* Through deck seams

Surface tension can allow water to cling to building materials instead of immediately falling vertically.

That is why leak diagnosis typically works **upslope from the interior stain**.

# Most Common Roof Leak Entry Points

## Plumbing Vent Boots

Common failures include:

* Cracked rubber collars
* UV deterioration
* Split gaskets
* Corroded flashing
* Improper shingle integration
* Exposed fasteners

Water can follow the pipe downward before appearing inside.

## Chimney Flashing

Inspect:

* Apron flashing
* Step flashing
* Counterflashing
* Cricket or saddle
* Masonry joints

A bead of sealant over failed flashing may slow water temporarily.

It does not necessarily rebuild the required water-shedding system.

## Roof Valleys

Valleys concentrate runoff from two roof planes.

Common leak causes include:

* Damaged valley lining
* Debris
* Incorrect cuts
* Poor nail placement
* Underlayment failure
* Ice
* Improper transitions

---

## Sidewalls and Headwalls

Potential problems include:

* Missing step flashing
* Failed kick-out flashing
* Siding installed too close to shingles
* Reused deteriorated flashing
* Wind-driven rain
* Sealant-dependent repairs

---

## Skylights

Water near a skylight can result from:

* Flashing failure
* Cladding problems
* Glazing seals
* Ice dams
* Condensation
* Debris

A ceiling stain beside a skylight does not automatically prove the skylight itself has failed.

# Safe Attic Leak Diagnosis

An accessible attic can help identify the water path.

Do not enter if:

* Severe weather is still occurring
* Electrical hazards are present
* Framing appears unstable
* Wet insulation hides safe footing
* There is no proper walking path
* Access would require stepping on drywall
* Water is entering electrical equipment

## Step 1: Locate the Interior Stain

Identify its position relative to:

* Ridge
* Exterior wall
* Chimney
* Valley
* Skylight
* Plumbing vent
* Dormer

---

## Step 2: Look Upslope

Trace toward the ridge rather than examining only the deck directly above the stain.

Wind-driven rain can complicate the path, but most gravity-driven flow progresses downslope after entry.

## Step 3: Follow Moisture Evidence

Look for:

* Wet wood
* Dark staining
* Water droplets
* Rusted nail points
* Reflective wet surfaces
* Damp insulation
* Mineral trails
* Stained framing

A wet rafter can lead directly toward the actual entry area.

## Step 4: Check Roof Penetrations

Pay particular attention to:

* Plumbing vents
* Chimneys
* Skylights
* Exhaust vents
* Roof vents
* Electrical mast penetrations

# Roof Leak or Attic Condensation?

Not every wet attic has an exterior roof leak.

## A Roof Leak More Often Shows:

* Localized staining
* A defined water trail
* Connection to rain
* Wetness near a penetration
* Moisture concentrated downslope

## Condensation More Often Shows:

* Distributed droplets
* Multiple wet nail tips
* Widespread sheathing moisture
* Frost during cold weather
* Moisture without active rainfall

Roof sealant will not fix excess attic humidity, air leakage, or inadequate ventilation.

Correct diagnosis matters.

# What Wet Roof Decking Looks Like

Temporary wetting does not automatically mean the deck must be replaced.

Structural condition matters.

## Plywood Warning Signs

Look for:

* Veneer separation
* Delamination
* Soft areas
* Swelling
* Sagging
* Fastener deterioration

## OSB Warning Signs

Look for:

* Swollen edges
* Flaking strands
* Soft surfaces
* Persistent thickness increase
* Poor fastener holding
* Sagging

Wet-but-intact decking can sometimes dry.

Soft, deteriorated, delaminated, or structurally inadequate decking is a different condition.

# How Professional Emergency Tarping Works

The following explains what a trained roofing crew may do.

It is **not an instruction for an unprotected homeowner to climb onto a damaged roof**.

## 1. Verify the Roof Is Structurally Accessible

Before walking the roof, the crew should evaluate:

* Deck condition
* Tree impacts
* Framing damage
* Electrical hazards
* Roof pitch
* Surface conditions
* Weather
* Safe access
* Fall-protection options

---

## 2. Identify the Damage Zone

The covering must extend beyond the visible opening.

A tarp ending directly beside the puncture can still allow wind-driven water underneath.

---

## 3. Extend Protection Upslope

Water should flow **over**, not behind, the temporary covering.

Damage near the ridge may require the covering to extend across or beyond the ridge rather than terminating in the main drainage path.

---

## 4. Use Reinforced Tarp Material

Thin plastic can tear around fasteners and grommets.

Professional emergency coverings typically use heavier reinforced material capable of tolerating greater tension and abrasion.

---

## 5. Spread Attachment Loads

Battens or dimensional lumber can be used to distribute clamping force across an edge.

This is more secure than depending entirely on isolated tarp grommets.

---

## 6. Secure the Tarp Against Wind

Loose bricks, concrete blocks, and random heavy objects are not reliable high-wind roof anchors.

They can:

* Move
* Fall
* Damage roofing
* Become projectiles

Mechanical attachment may intentionally penetrate roofing in an area that will later require repair.

Those penetrations should be included in the permanent repair scope.

---

## 7. Preserve Drainage

A tarp should not create:

* Reverse laps
* Water pockets
* Troughs
* Ponding
* Dams
* Runoff behind siding

Water-shedding geometry still matters on a temporary roof covering.

# Plywood and OSB Emergency Board-Ups: What Homeowners Should Know

Storm damage can leave openings too large for a tarp alone.

Professional crews may use plywood or OSB to temporarily secure an opening before applying weather protection.

> **OSB / Plywood Trigger:** The IRC does not provide one universal homeowner specification for emergency roof board-ups. Temporary structural covering must be appropriate for the opening, securely supported, and later incorporated into a code-compliant permanent repair.

Temporary OSB or plywood should **not** be confused with completed roof decking or a permanent roof covering.

When a branch or impact has broken the deck, the permanent repair may require:

* Removal of damaged roofing
* Removal of compromised decking
* New structural sheathing
* Correct panel thickness
* Proper edge support where required
* Correct fastening
* Underlayment
* New roof covering

The exact requirements depend on the adopted code and the structure.

# When Temporary Repairs Reveal Widespread Deck Rot

This is where an emergency leak can turn into a much larger project.

Suppose a roofer removes shingles around a storm puncture and finds:

* Soft OSB extending well beyond the impact
* Delamination
* Long-term staining
* Old leaks
* Widespread fastener failure
* Decay around multiple penetrations

That condition needs to be documented separately from the storm opening.

## IRC R908 Does Not Automatically Make Old Rot an Insurance Loss

This distinction matters.

Building code may determine **how repair or replacement must be performed**.

Insurance determines **what portion of that scope is covered under the policy**.

Existing deterioration, wear, poor maintenance, or long-term leakage may be treated differently from sudden storm damage.

### Ask the Contractor to Separate:

* Direct storm damage
* Resulting water damage
* Pre-existing deterioration
* Code-required work
* Recommended upgrades

This makes both the contractor scope and insurance claim easier to evaluate.

# RCV vs. ACV: Why an Emergency Leak Can Create a Large Cash-Flow Gap

This is one of the most important insurance issues for an older roof.

## Replacement Cost Value — RCV

RCV generally values covered repairs at current replacement cost without permanently deducting depreciation, subject to policy terms.

Some policies initially issue an ACV-type payment and release **recoverable depreciation** after qualifying repairs are completed and documented.

## Actual Cash Value — ACV

ACV generally subtracts depreciation for age, condition, and wear.

### Illustrative Example

Assume:

* Covered roof replacement: **$18,000**
* Depreciation: **$8,000**
* Deductible: **$2,500**

An illustrative ACV calculation could be:

`$18,000 - $8,000 - $2,500 = $7,500`

That can leave a major homeowner funding gap.

Under qualifying RCV coverage, recoverable depreciation may change the final settlement after repairs are completed.

Policy language controls.

# Temporary Repair vs. Permanent Roof Repair

Do not allow an emergency tarp to become an indefinite roof system.

| Temporary mitigation            | Permanent correction                   |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Tarp over missing shingles      | Replace damaged roof covering          |
| Temporary plywood over puncture | Replace compromised deck and roofing   |
| Bucket under interior drip      | Eliminate exterior water entry         |
| Temporary pipe-boot cover       | Replace failed pipe flashing           |
| Emergency sealant               | Rebuild defective flashing/detail      |
| Plastic interior diversion      | Repair roof and dry affected materials |

A roof covered with emergency sealant is not necessarily permanently repaired.

# How Long Can a Roof Tarp Stay On?

There is no universal safe lifespan.

Temporary tarps deteriorate because of:

* UV exposure
* Wind cycling
* Abrasion
* Fastener enlargement
* Ponding
* Branches
* Snow
* Ice
* Edge lifting

Some properly installed coverings may remain effective for weeks or longer.

Severe weather can damage one overnight.

Inspect temporary roof protection after subsequent storms and schedule permanent repair as soon as practical.

# Drying the House After the Roof Is Covered

Stopping rainwater is only half of the mitigation process.

Wet building materials must also dry.

EPA guidance recommends drying wet or damp building materials **within approximately 24–48 hours where practical** to reduce the chance of mold growth.

## Structural Drying May Require:

* Water extraction
* Dehumidification
* Controlled air movement
* Wet insulation removal
* Ceiling removal
* Opening wall or ceiling cavities
* Moisture monitoring

### Do Not Seal Wet Cavities

Avoid immediately:

* Painting wet drywall
* Closing wet ceilings
* Reinstalling wet insulation
* Covering damp decking
* Encapsulating visible microbial growth

The assembly needs to dry.

---

# The "24–48 Hour Mold Rule" Is a Response Target, Not a Stopwatch

Do not interpret the 24–48-hour recommendation as meaning a house is safe at hour 47 and suddenly contaminated at hour 49.

Actual microbial growth depends on:

* Temperature
* Humidity
* Material
* Water source
* Duration
* Air movement
* Existing spores

The practical lesson is:

**Start drying immediately.**

Do not wait for visible mold before addressing saturation.

---

# Wet Insulation Often Requires Special Attention

Saturated insulation can:

* Hold water against wood
* Increase ceiling weight
* Slow drying
* Lose thermal effectiveness
* Conceal wet framing

Some wet insulation can be difficult to dry effectively in place.

Removal may be appropriate depending on:

* Insulation type
* Water source
* Duration
* Contamination
* Degree of saturation

Document removal before disposal when an insurance claim is active.

# Storm Damage Protocol: Wind, Hail, and Falling Trees

## After Wind or Hail

From the ground, document:

* Missing shingles
* Lifted shingles
* Metal damage
* Gutter dents
* Broken vents
* Ridge damage
* Fallen debris

Do not walk a storm-damaged roof merely to search for hail marks.

## After a Tree or Large Branch Impact

Treat the affected area as potentially structural.

Look for:

* Roof sagging
* Broken rafters
* Split trusses
* Cracked ceilings
* Wall movement
* Broken electrical service equipment

Do not remove a large tree from the roof until contractors determine whether its weight is affecting structural stability.

Removing the load can sometimes change how damaged framing behaves.

## Snow and Ice Regions

A winter leak may be caused by:

* Ice dams
* Snow intrusion
* Flashing problems
* Condensation
* Freeze-thaw cycling

Do not climb onto an icy roof to remove snow or install a tarp.

Interior diagnosis and professional exterior mitigation are safer starting points.

After significant hail:

* Photograph damage from safe locations
* Preserve storm timing
* Check gutters and exterior metals
* Document interior leaks
* Avoid unnecessary roof traffic

An active leak can provide evidence of functional damage, but the complete cause still requires inspection.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Should I Tarp My Roof While It Is Still Raining?

Homeowners should not climb onto a wet roof during an active storm.

Rain, lightning, hail, high wind, and wet roofing all increase fall risk.

A tarp can also become extremely difficult to control when wind gets beneath it.

During the storm:

* Control interior water
* Clear unsafe rooms
* Protect belongings
* Document conditions
* Call an emergency contractor

Exterior work should begin when the roof and weather conditions allow trained personnel to work safely.

---

## Will Homeowners Insurance Pay for Emergency Roof Tarping?

It may.

Homeowners policies commonly require reasonable measures to protect property from additional damage after a loss.

If the underlying event is covered, reasonable temporary roof protection may be included in the claim.

However, payment depends on:

* Policy terms
* Cause of damage
* Deductible
* Limits
* Exclusions
* Reasonableness of the expense

Save invoices, photographs, and receipts.

---

## Will Insurance Cover a Roof Leak Caused by an Old Roof?

Not automatically.

Insurance typically distinguishes between a sudden covered event and deterioration caused by:

* Age
* Wear
* Maintenance problems
* Long-term leakage
* Rot
* Installation defects

For example, a storm-created opening may be treated differently from a roof that has slowly leaked because flashing deteriorated over several years.

Policy terms control.

---

## What Are the Signs a Wet Ceiling Could Collapse?

Warning signs include:

* Rapidly expanding bulge
* Significant downward bowing
* Cracking
* Opening drywall seams
* Fasteners pulling through drywall
* Falling gypsum
* Audible cracking
* Saturated insulation falling through openings
* Increasing water volume
* Visible framing movement

Remember that **10 gallons of water weighs about 83 pounds** before adding saturated drywall and insulation.

If the ceiling is visibly deforming, keep people out of the room.

---

## What Should I Do if Water Is Coming Through a Light Fixture?

Do not touch the fixture.

Keep people away from the wet area.

If the circuit can be safely shut off from a completely dry electrical panel without entering the hazard area, it may be appropriate to de-energize it.

If the panel is wet, the floor is wet, arcing is present, or you cannot identify the circuit safely, contact qualified electrical or emergency assistance.

Do not restore power to water-exposed electrical equipment until it has been evaluated.

## How Much Does Emergency Roof Tarping Cost in 2026?

Professional tarping commonly costs approximately:

**$0.70–$2.00 per square foot**

Emergency and after-hours tarping can cost around:

**$1.00–$2.80 per square foot**

A typical project may cost several hundred dollars, while large or complex emergency coverings can reach several thousand.

Roof pitch, access, storm demand, tarp size, structural damage, and after-hours service all affect price.

## How Much Does It Cost to Repair a Roof Leak in 2026?

Broad planning ranges are:

* **Minor leak:** $150–$1,000
* **Moderate leak:** $1,000–$3,000
* **Major leak with structural/interior damage:** $3,000–$6,000+

Some isolated shingle repairs cost less.

Structural damage, extensive decking replacement, mold remediation, and major interior reconstruction can cost much more.

---

## Can a Roof Tarp Cause an Insurance Claim Denial?

A properly documented reasonable temporary repair generally serves the opposite purpose: it helps prevent additional damage.

Problems can arise when homeowners make extensive permanent changes before the loss is documented or destroy evidence needed to determine cause.

The safer sequence is:

**Document → mitigate → preserve records → notify insurer → permanently repair.**

Never delay emergency water containment merely to wait for an adjuster when delay would allow additional damage.

---

## Does Finding Rotten Roof Decking Mean Insurance Must Replace My Entire Roof?

No.

Deck rot establishes a condition that may require repair or replacement for structural and code reasons.

It does not automatically establish that the insurer owes for the deterioration.

The contractor should distinguish:

* Storm-created damage
* Resulting water damage
* Pre-existing deterioration
* Code-required work
* Optional improvements

If code compliance increases covered reconstruction costs, whether those additional costs are insured can depend on the policy's ordinance-or-law provisions and other terms.

---

## Does the IRC 25% Rule Require Full Roof Replacement?

There is **no universal nationwide IRC 25% rule** requiring complete residential roof replacement whenever more than 25% is damaged.

IRC R908 establishes reroofing requirements, while states and local jurisdictions can adopt their own amendments and thresholds.

Ask the local building department or contractor for the exact adopted code section before relying on a 25% rule.

---

# Bottom Line: What Matters Most During an Active Roof Leak

During an emergency, do not worry first about whether the roof needs three shingles or complete replacement.

Deal with the crisis in this order:

1. **Protect people from electrical and collapse hazards.**
2. **Contain interior water where safe.**
3. **Document storm and water damage.**
4. **Keep homeowners off unsafe roofs.**
5. **Use secure professional temporary protection when needed.**
6. **Dry wet materials promptly.**
7. **Diagnose the actual water-entry point.**
8. **Separate emergency mitigation from permanent repair.**
9. **Verify local code requirements instead of assuming a nationwide 25% rule.**
10. **Compare the contractor scope with the insurance estimate line by line.**

A bucket stops water from reaching the floor.

A tarp may stop the next rainfall.

But the emergency is not over until the **entry point is permanently repaired, hidden wet materials are evaluated, and the structure is dry enough for continued service**.
